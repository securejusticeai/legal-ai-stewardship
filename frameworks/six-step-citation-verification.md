# The Six-Step Citation Verification Protocol
## Canonical Framework | AI Stewardship | Track 1: Legal & Organizational

---

## Definition

The Six-Step Citation Verification Protocol is the explicit procedure for preventing AI hallucination sanctions in legal practice. It exists because the hallucination problem is not a mystery — every major legal AI company has disclosed it, the ABA has issued guidance on it, state bars have published warnings, and judges have made examples of violators in published opinions. And attorneys keep submitting fake citations to courts.

*"The hallucination is not the failure. The unsupervised hallucination reaching a court is the failure."* — Richard Schreiber

---

## The Six Steps

### Step 1 — Mental Model Shift
Treat every AI-generated citation as unverified. Not probably correct. Not likely accurate. Unverified — until Step 6 is complete.

This is not a procedural step. It is a professional posture. The attorney who approaches an AI-generated citation as probably fine and checks it casually is one distracted afternoon away from a sanctions hearing. The attorney who treats it as unverified until proven otherwise has already eliminated the failure mode that produced 1,397 documented hallucination cases in U.S. courts (Damien Charlotin AI Hallucination Cases Database, damiencharlotin.com/hallucinations — this count changes daily; re-check the live database before citing).

### Step 2 — Independent Database Verification
Run every citation through Westlaw or LexisNexis. The case must exist, the caption must match, and the citation must resolve to the actual opinion. The AI's summary of the case is not the case.

### Step 3 — Shepardize or KeyCite
Confirm the authority is still good law. A real case that has been overturned, superseded, or negatively treated is a different failure than a fabricated one — but it is still a failure in front of a judge.

### Step 4 — Written Verification Log
Create a written record of who verified each citation, when, and against which database. The log is the evidence that the firm's human-in-the-loop standard operated. If a question ever reaches a court or a carrier, the log is the difference between an error and negligence.

### Step 5 — Separate Reviewer
Designate a reviewer who is not the drafter. The attorney who generated the brief cannot be the only set of eyes that verifies it. Fresh eyes catch what authorship blindness misses.

### Step 6 — Disclose AI Use Where Required
Know each court's standing orders and each jurisdiction's disclosure requirements, and disclose AI use where required. Disclosure rules are proliferating court by court — the protocol treats checking them as a step, not an afterthought.
